# BathymetryReferences
References for poster of bathymetry analysis

**Cited text in the poster**

**Introduction**

- The nearshore area is highly dynamic: waves, tides, currents and storms redistribute sediment and reshape the seabed \[1\].
- Repeated bathymetric surveys track these changes and support coastal engineering and management \[2\].
- Acoustic surveys derive depth from sound-wave travel time. Interpolation turns discrete soundings into a surface (Fig 1.), but measurement uncertainty and uneven coverage limit interpretation \[3, 4\].
- Survey area conditions \[5\].
- Main Drivers \[6, 7\].

**Discussion**

- Largely stable seabed: Most differences fall within survey uncertainty (0.5m), so net erosion or accretion cannot be confirmed (Table 1). The survey depths lie below the estimated zonal depth of closure ≈ 10,4 m \[8\], where little change is expected over six years.
- Localized differences: Larger discrepancies, notably profile F (Fig.2), may reflect the nearby Faro-Olhão inlet \[9\], interpolation edge effects or processing errors \[10\] and need verification (Fig.3).
- Drivers: Waves, storms and currents redistribute nearshore sediment; inlet currents add local variability. Two surveys alone cannot identify the dominant driver \[11\].
- Limitations: Two snapshots show net difference, not timing: post-storm erosion and recovery may go undetected \[6\]. Single-beam vs multibeam coverage, interpolation on low coverage cells and tide/datum corrections could be increasing uncertainty.
